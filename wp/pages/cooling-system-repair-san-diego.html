Key Components of Your Vehicle's Cooling System

Your engine operates at internal temperatures exceeding 200°F. The cooling system circulates liquid coolant through engine passages to absorb heat, releasing it safely into the atmosphere through the radiator core.

  • Radiator: Transfers fluid heat to the incoming outside air.
  • Water Pump: Circulates coolant through the engine block, heater core, and hoses.
  • Thermostat: Regulates coolant flow based on operating engine temperature.
  • Cooling Fans: Pull air through the radiator fins when idling or driving at low speeds.

Warning signs

Signs Your Vehicle is Overheating

Any of these means it is worth getting checked before it becomes a bigger repair.

  • The temperature gauge needle creeps into the red zone or high warning area.
  • Steam or white smoke pouring out from under the hood.
  • Sweet-smelling steam inside the cabin or sweet green/pink fluid puddling under the front bumper.
  • Heater blowing cold air even when the engine temperature reads high.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if my car starts overheating while driving?

Safely pull over to the shoulder immediately, turn off the engine, and DO NOT open the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Call for roadside service or towing to avoid catastrophic engine block damage.

Why is my car leaking coolant?

Coolant leaks commonly originate from brittle rubber hoses, cracked plastic radiator end tanks, leaking water pump seals, or loose hose clamps.

How often should I flush my engine coolant?

Most modern vehicles require a coolant flush every 30,000 to 60,000 miles or every 3 to 5 years, as protective anti-corrosion additives in the fluid wear out over time.
