The Importance of Transmission Fluid Services

Transmission fluid cleans, cools, and hydraulically operates the internal clutch packs and shift solenoids inside your gearbox. Over time, high operating temperatures oxidize fluid, causing it to lose viscosity and build up dark varnish. Routine transmission fluid flushes or pan service removes contaminated fluid and metal shavings, preventing delayed gear engagement, harsh shifting, and premature internal gear failure.

Diagnostic Precision for Complex Issues

Transmission issues often present alarming symptoms like slipping or hard shifts, but the underlying root cause can range from a simple low fluid level or bad sensor to internal mechanical failure. Our shop uses advanced diagnostic scanners to read transmission control unit (TCU) fault codes, test shift solenoid performance, and analyze fluid condition before recommending any major service.

Warning signs

Warning Signs of Transmission Problems

Any of these means it is worth getting checked before it becomes a bigger repair.

  • Slipping Gears: Engine RPM surges high, but the vehicle hesitates or fails to accelerate properly.
  • Rough or Delayed Shifts: Hard clunking or hesitation when shifting between Park, Drive, and Reverse.
  • Fluid Leaks: Bright red, dark brown, or pink fluid puddles forming beneath your vehicle.
  • Burning Smell: A harsh sweet or burnt metallic odor coming from under the hood.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How often should I change my transmission fluid?

Most manufacturers recommend replacing transmission fluid every 30,000 to 60,000 miles for standard driving, or sooner if towing heavy loads or driving extensively in city stop-and-go traffic.

What causes a transmission to slip?

Transmission slippage is commonly caused by low fluid levels, worn clutch plates, degraded fluid viscosity, or failing solenoid valves that control hydraulic pressure inside the unit.

Can I drive my car if the transmission light is on?

It is not recommended. Driving with active transmission fault codes or low fluid pressure can rapidly turn a minor sensor fix into a major, costly transmission overhaul.
