Complete Brake System Inspection & Maintenance

Brake components wear down naturally over time due to friction and heat. Ignoring early warning signs can lead to damaged rotors, compromised stopping distances, and higher repair bills. Our certified mechanics conduct thorough digital brake inspections covering every element of your stopping system. We check friction material thickness, rotor surface condition, caliper hydraulic operation, brake line integrity, and moisture levels in your brake fluid.

Comprehensive Repair Solutions

  • Brake Pad Replacement: Installation of ceramic or semi-metallic friction pads engineered for low noise, minimal dust, and maximum stopping power.

Warning signs

Common Warning Signs of Brake Wear

Any of these means it is worth getting checked before it becomes a bigger repair.

  • Squealing, grinding, or high-pitched metallic noises when applying pressure to the pedal.
  • Spongy, soft, or low brake pedal feel requiring extra foot pressure to stop.
  • Steering wheel vibration or brake pedal pulsation during deceleration.
  • The vehicle pulls to one side when stopping.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I get my brakes inspected or replaced?

We recommend inspecting your brakes every 6,000 to 10,000 miles, typically during routine oil changes. Brake pads generally last between 30,000 and 70,000 miles depending on your driving habits, terrain, and vehicle weight.

Why are my brakes making a grinding noise?

A loud grinding sound usually means the friction material on your brake pads has worn away completely, causing the metal backing plate to scrape directly against the brake rotor. This requires immediate service to prevent rotor destruction.

What causes the steering wheel to shake when I hit the brakes?

Vibration during braking is most commonly caused by uneven rotor wear or thermal warping. Machining or replacing the rotors restores a flat contact surface and smooth braking performance.
