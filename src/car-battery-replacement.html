Warning signs

Is it the battery, or something else?

A slow crank is the obvious one. These are the symptoms drivers usually blame on something more expensive — and half the time it's a dying battery.

Slow, laboured crank

The engine turns over sluggishly before it catches, especially on the first start of the morning.

Dim lights at idle

Headlights and dash lights brighten when you rev. That's the charging system working around a weak battery.

Random electrical faults

Windows crawling, radio resetting, warning lights appearing and clearing. Low voltage confuses modern control modules.

Needed a jump start

Once is a warning. A battery that has been fully flattened rarely recovers its original capacity.

Swollen case or corrosion

A bulging case means heat damage. Furry blue-white build-up on the terminals means it's venting acid.

Over three years old

San Diego heat is hard on batteries. Past three years you're on borrowed time — get it tested, free.

Battery types

We stock the battery your car actually takes

Fitting the wrong chemistry is the fastest way to kill a new battery. Here's what goes in what.

01

Standard flooded

Older domestic and Japanese vehicles without stop-start. Reliable and the most affordable option where it's the correct fitment.

02

EFB

Enhanced flooded, built for entry-level stop-start systems. Handles far more charge cycles than a standard battery.

03

AGM

Absorbent glass mat — required on most European models, stop-start vehicles and anything with a heavy electrical load.

04

Hybrid & EV 12V

Hybrids and EVs still run a 12V auxiliary battery, and when it fails the car often won't power up at all. We're hybrid certified.

FAQ

Car battery questions we get asked

How long does a car battery replacement take?

Most batteries are tested and replaced in about 30 to 45 minutes. European vehicles and models with the battery under a seat or in the trunk take longer, because the new battery has to be registered to the charging system.

How much does a car battery cost in San Diego?

It depends on the group size and chemistry your vehicle needs — an AGM battery for a European car costs more than a standard flooded battery. We test first, tell you exactly which battery your car takes, and quote parts and fitting before any work starts.

How long should a car battery last?

Three to five years is typical, and San Diego heat shortens that. Heat degrades a battery faster than cold does. We test your battery free at every visit, so you find out it's on the way out before it strands you.

Does my new battery need to be registered or coded?

Most BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, MINI and Volkswagen models do. Without registration the charging system keeps charging to the old battery's profile, which overcharges the new one and kills it early. We register it as part of the job.

My car needed a jump start — do I definitely need a new battery?

Not necessarily. It could be the alternator not charging, or a parasitic drain pulling current while the car is parked. That's exactly why we test all three before recommending anything.

Do you take the old battery away?

Yes — the old battery goes for certified recycling at no charge to you.
